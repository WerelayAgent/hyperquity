Launch a coin. Open a position.

Hyperquity launches coins on Float that each run one leveraged position on Hyperliquid. Each coin's dev share of Float's trading fee becomes margin for that position. When it makes money, the profit buys the coin back and burns it. This page explains every step, every number and every risk.

Start

Overview#

A Hyperquity coin is an ordinary Float coin. It is launched through Float's program on Raydium LaunchLab, trades on a bonding curve, graduates to a Raydium pool, and shows up in every wallet, explorer and terminal like any other. It is listed on Float too. Float splits every coin's trading fee on chain, and 5% of that split is a dev share paid to a wallet set at launch. On a Hyperquity coin, that wallet is the coin's own fee wallet, so the dev share funds a single position chosen at launch, for example long HYPE 3x or short ETH 5x, held in that coin's own Hyperliquid account.

That gives each coin three properties:

A thesis

The market, direction and leverage are fixed at launch and shown on the coin's page forever. Holders know exactly what the dev share is doing.

Volume that compounds

More trading means more fees, more margin and a bigger position. Hyperquity follows activity, not hype.

Profit that returns

When the position is up 20%, the gain is taken, bridged back to Solana and used to buy and burn the coin.

Everything happens in public: each claim, swap, bridge, order, profit-take, buyback and burn is logged on the coin's page with a link to the transaction.

The fee loop#

These steps run automatically for every coin, around the clock. The keeper checks every coin about every 30 seconds.

  1. 01

    Trade

    Someone buys or sells the coin, anywhere. Float charges the fee tier the launcher picked (1%, 2% or 2.5%) and splits it on chain; 5% of what Raydium leaves is the dev share, paid in the pair token.

  2. 02

    Claim

    The keeper calls Float's permissionless claim instructions, and the dev share lands in the coin's fee wallet in the pair token.

  3. 03

    Convert

    The pair token is turned into SOL: wSOL is unwrapped, anything else is swapped on Jupiter once it is worth at least 0.005 SOL.

  4. 04

    Bridge

    Once 0.13 SOL has built up it is sent through Unit, the bridge that carries SOL onto Hyperliquid, to the coin's Hyperliquid account.

  5. 05

    Margin

    On Hyperliquid the SOL (USOL) is sold for USDC and moved into the perp account. The position grows to match.

  6. 06

    Profit → burn

    At +20% the profit is taken, bridged back as SOL, used to buy the coin and burned.

Launch

Launching a coin#

A launch goes through the Float program from the Hyperquity launch form, in one wallet approval. It does three things:

  1. 01

    Creates the coin

    A Float coin on Raydium LaunchLab: 1,000,000,000 fixed supply, no mint or freeze authority, paired with the token you pick and charging the fee tier you pick. It is listed on Float as well as on Hyperquity.

  2. 02

    Sets the fee receiver

    Float pays each coin's dev share to the wallet set at launch. Hyperquity sets it to a fee wallet derived for this coin alone, not your wallet. That is what lets the dev share run the position.

  3. 03

    Buys, if you asked

    An optional dev buy, paid in SOL, in the same approval. For a SOL pair the SOL is wrapped and spent on the curve; for any other pair Jupiter swaps SOL into the pair token first. The coins go to your wallet.

Float's coin mechanics

  • Fixed supply of 1,000,000,000, with no mint or freeze authority.
  • Starting market cap around $20,000.
  • A bonding curve that graduates at about 7.5x, roughly $150,000.
  • On graduation, liquidity moves to a Raydium pool and is locked forever with Raydium Burn & Earn.

Tickers

A ticker can be launched once for free; case doesn't matter. Reusing a ticker costs 1 SOL, which Float charges and sends to its treasury. The Hyperquity form warns you before that happens.

Fixed at launch

The pair, fee tier, market, direction and leverage cannot be changed afterwards, by you or by Hyperquity. Neither can the fee receiver: the dev share always goes to the position.

Pair and fee tier#

Pair

A coin can be paired with any of the 400 tokens Float supports: SOL, USDC, tokenized stocks from xStocks and Sunrise, pre-IPO tokens, ZEC and XMR, memecoins and more. The pair is what the curve and pool trade against, and it is the token every fee is paid in, including the dev share. The keeper converts the dev share to SOL before bridging, whatever the pair.

Fee tier

Float charges one of three fees on every buy and sell: 1%, 2% or 2.5%. The dev share is 5% of what is left after Raydium's 0.25%, so as a share of volume it is:

Fee tierDev share of volumePer $10,000 traded
1%0.0375%$3.75
2%0.0875%$8.75
2.5%0.1125%$11.25

Positions build slowly

The dev share is a small slice of each trade: 0.0375% at 1%, 0.0875% at 2% and 0.1125% at 2.5% of volume. A position only grows quickly if its coin trades a lot. A higher tier builds it faster, and costs traders more.

Choosing a position#

Market

Any Hyperliquid perpetual that supports cross margin: majors like BTC, ETH and SOL, Hyperliquid's own HYPE, and over a hundred more. The launch page lists them all live, with price, 24h change, volume and each market's maximum leverage.

Direction

Long profits when the market goes up. Short profits when it goes down. Funding is paid or received like any Hyperliquid position.

Leverage

From 1x up to 20x, or the market's own maximum if lower. Leverage sets how far the market can move against the position before it is liquidated. Roughly:

LeverageLiquidated by a move of aboutCharacter
1xnever (fully collateralised)patient
2x50% againstpatient
3x33% againstbalanced
5x20% againstbalanced
10x10.0% againstaggressive
20x5.0% againstvery aggressive

Maintenance margin makes the real distance a little shorter. New fees add margin over time, which pushes the liquidation price further away.

Mechanics

Float's fee split#

Float enforces the split on chain, and it is the same for every coin. While a coin is on its curve, Raydium keeps 0.25% of each trade. Float splits the rest of the fee:

ShareGoes toPaid in
15%Holders of the coinThe pair token
5%Dev share: the wallet set at launch (on Hyperquity, the coin's fee wallet)The pair token
35%Buying back and burning $FLOAT-
35%Top traders by volumeUSDC
10%Float's treasury-

Only the 5% dev share passes through Hyperquity. In practice that is (tier − 0.25%) × 5% of volume: 0.0375% at 1%, 0.0875% at 2% and 0.1125% at 2.5%. The rest is Float's, and its own docs describe it.

Fees to margin#

Every Hyperquity coin has its own fee wallet as its Float fee receiver, so each wallet holds exactly one coin's dev share. Nothing is pooled between coins.

  1. 01

    Claim

    At most about every 10 minutes, when there is something to claim, the keeper calls Float's permissionless claim_coin_fees and then claim_dev_fees. The dev share lands in the fee wallet in the pair token. Float's own bot also cycles fees; either way the share arrives.

  2. 02

    Convert

    wSOL is unwrapped. Any other pair token is swapped to SOL on Jupiter once it is worth at least 0.005 SOL.

  3. 03

    Bridge

    At 0.13 SOL the fees are sent to the coin's Unit deposit address. Unit's minimum is 0.12 SOL; the extra covers its fee. Unit credits it to the coin's Hyperliquid account as USOL.

  4. 04

    Swap

    The USOL is sold for USDC on Hyperliquid spot.

  5. 05

    Deposit

    The USDC moves from spot into the perp account. It now counts as margin and raises the basis.

Why a minimum

Bridging has a fixed cost, so small amounts wait. With the dev share at 0.0375% at 1%, 0.0875% at 2% and 0.1125% at 2.5% of volume, a coin needs real trading before its first position opens; on a quiet coin that can take a long time.

Sizing the position#

Every coin tracks a basis: the USDC that has gone into its account and is still working. The position is sized to:

target notional = min(equity, basis) × leverage
  • New fees raise the basis, so the position grows with trading volume.
  • Unrealized gains never add size. A winning position is not pyramided.
  • After a loss the target shrinks with equity, so a drawdown is never re-levered.
  • Orders under $11 are skipped; Hyperliquid's minimum is $10.
  • Orders are market orders, filled within 2% of the mid price.

Taking profit#

Profit is measured as equity above basis. It is taken when both are true:

equity − basis ≥ 20% of basis and ≥ $25

Then, in one pass:

  1. 01

    Trim

    The position is reduced back to basis × leverage, which realizes the gain.

  2. 02

    Withdraw

    The profit moves from the perp account to spot. The basis stays where it was.

  3. 03

    Bridge back

    The USDC is swapped to SOL and sent through Unit to the coin's buyback wallet on Solana.

Because the basis doesn't move, the next profit-take is measured from the same line: the position always keeps working on the fees that funded it.

Buyback and burn#

When SOL lands in the buyback wallet, the keeper buys the coin with it:

  • Through Jupiter, which routes to the coin's LaunchLab curve while it is bonding and to its Raydium pool after it graduates.
  • A small reserve (0.01 SOL) stays behind to pay the wallet's own transaction fees.

The bought coins are then burned: destroyed on chain, reducing total supply. Both transactions appear in the activity feed, and the running total is shown under “Where the fees go”.

Losses and liquidation#

Leverage loses money as readily as it makes it. If the market moves against the position, equity falls. If it falls far enough, Hyperliquid liquidates the position and most of the margin is lost.

When a coin's account ends up flat with less than its basis, the basis is reset to what is left. The loop then simply continues: new fees become new margin and reopen the same trade.

Example · short SOL 10x

Basis

position liquidated, equity ≈ $0
Basis reset
$2,000 → $0 (logged as “Basis reset”)
Next $150 of fees

No profit, no burn

Buybacks only ever come from realized profit. A coin whose position never reaches +20% will never buy back. No one tops up a losing position except future fees.

Using Hyperquity

Trading a coin#

Every coin page has a buy and sell panel that routes through Jupiter: to the LaunchLab curve while the coin is bonding, and to its Raydium pool once it graduates. You can also trade Hyperquity coins on Float or any terminal, such as Axiom, GMGN or Jupiter. Every trade pays the fee wherever it happens, so every trade feeds the position.

  • Slippage defaults to 5%; choose 2%, 5% or 10% on the panel.
  • Your wallet signs; Hyperquity broadcasts through its own RPC and confirms the transaction.
  • The bonding badge on each card shows how full the curve is.

Wallets and custody#

Each coin gets its own fee wallet, buyback wallet and Hyperliquid account, derived by Hyperquity's keeper for that coin alone. Nothing is pooled across coins.

WalletChainRole
Fee walletSolanaFloat fee receiver. Receives the dev share, converts it to SOL and sends it to Hyperliquid.
Hyperliquid accountHyperliquidHolds the margin and the position. Public on the Hyperliquid explorer.
Buyback walletSolanaReceives profit back from Hyperliquid, buys the coin and burns it.

The Hyperquity keeper signs for these wallets and pays the Solana network fees for claims, swaps, bridges and burns. That makes Hyperquity custodial over them: you are trusting the operator's keys and code rather than a smart contract. Every address is public, so every movement can be checked.

Risks#

  • Leverage. A position can lose part or all of its margin. Most leveraged positions eventually lose money.
  • Liquidation. A fast move against the position can wipe it out before new fees arrive.
  • Slow build. The dev share is a small part of each trade. Unless a coin trades a lot, its position stays small.
  • Delays. Profit is only bought back after it is realized and bridged. That takes minutes, not seconds.
  • Float. Float's program is unaudited and upgradeable by its team. A change on their side could break Hyperquity's integration.
  • Tokenized stocks. They are issued by third parties that can pause or claw back their tokens, which stops trading and fees for coins paired with them.
  • Pre-IPO tokens. Some charge a fee on every transfer.
  • ZEC and XMR. On Solana they are bridged tokens, with the bridge's risks.
  • Third parties. Float, Raydium, Hyperliquid, Unit and Jupiter can pause, change or fail.
  • Custody. The keeper holds the keys. A bug or a compromise of the operator can lose funds.
  • Experimental. Hyperquity is new and unaudited.
  • No affiliation. Hyperquity is not affiliated with Float or Hyperliquid, or with the companies behind tokenized stocks. A coin is not a share.
  • Not an investment. None of this is a promise of returns or a security offering.

FAQ#

Can I change the position after launch?+

No. The market, side and leverage are fixed for the life of the coin, and so are the pair and fee tier. That is the point: holders know the thesis can't be swapped.

Do I, as launcher, earn the dev share?+

No. Hyperquity points every coin's dev share at that coin's own fee wallet, and it funds the position. If you want exposure to your coin, use the dev buy.

Which fee tier should I pick?+

A higher tier builds the position faster (0.0375% at 1%, 0.0875% at 2% and 0.1125% at 2.5% of volume) but costs every trader more.

When does the first position open?+

Once 0.13 SOL of dev share has built up and reached Hyperliquid. That takes real trading volume; on a quiet coin it can take a long time.

What happens if the position is liquidated?+

The basis resets to what is left and future fees reopen the same trade. Past burns are permanent.

Can I see the position myself?+

Yes. Every coin page links its Hyperliquid account; the position is public on the Hyperliquid explorer.

What if the coin graduates?+

Nothing changes for the loop. Liquidity moves to a Raydium pool, locked with Burn & Earn, and trades and buybacks route there through Jupiter.

Does trading elsewhere count?+

Yes. Every trade pays the fee wherever it happens: on Hyperquity, on Float, or on Axiom, GMGN, Jupiter and other terminals.

Where do announcements go?+

Hyperquity on X. Anything else claiming to be Hyperquity is not.

Glossary#

Basis
USDC that has gone into a coin's account and is still working. The position is sized on it.
Hyperquity
The account's value on Hyperliquid: margin plus unrealized PnL.
Notional
The position's size in dollars: size × mark price.
Mark price
Hyperliquid's fair price for the market, used for PnL and liquidation.
ROE
Return on equity: unrealized PnL as a share of margin.
Float
The launchpad Hyperquity coins are created through. Its program splits every coin's fee on chain.
Pair
The token a coin trades against and is paid fees in.
Fee tier
The fee Float charges on every buy and sell: 1%, 2% or 2.5%.
Dev share
5% of Float's split, paid to the wallet set at launch. On Hyperquity, it funds the position.
Bonding curve
Raydium LaunchLab's launch pricing. When it fills, the coin graduates to a Raydium pool.
Unit
A bridge that brings SOL, BTC and others onto Hyperliquid as spot tokens (USOL, UBTC).
Keeper
The Hyperquity process that runs the fee loop for every coin.
